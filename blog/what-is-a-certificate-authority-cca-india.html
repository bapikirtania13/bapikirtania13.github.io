What is a Certifying Authority? Understanding CCA India and Trusted Certificates

✍️ Kirtania 📅 Published: November 3, 2026 ⏱️ 6 min read 🏷️ Legal & Cryptographic Framework

When you receive an official digitally signed document in India—such as an e-Aadhaar card from UIDAI, a digitally signed income tax assessment, a corporate e-filing on the MCA21 portal, or a PAN card confirmation—you may notice names like "CCA India", "e-Mudhra", or "NSDL" in the certificate properties.

What role do these organizations play in verifying identity? In this guide, we break down the legal and technical foundation of Certifying Authorities (CAs) and the Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA) under the Information Technology Act, 2000.

1. The Trust Problem in Digital Signatures

Public-key cryptography allows anyone to generate a pair of keys: a private key used to sign data, and a public key used by anyone to verify the signature. However, this system has an inherent flaw: anyone can generate a key pair and attach the name "UIDAI" or "Government of India" to it.

To prevent impersonation, we need a legally recognized, heavily audited third party to verify an individual or organization's physical identity before issuing a cryptographic certificate that binds that identity to their public key. This trusted third party is a Certifying Authority.

2. What is a Certifying Authority?

A Certifying Authority (CA) is an organization licensed to issue Digital Signature Certificates (DSC). A Digital Signature Certificate acts as a cryptographic passport: it contains the holder's legal name, organization, public key, certificate expiration date, and the cryptographic digital signature of the issuing CA.

Under Section 24 of India's Information Technology Act, 2000, no entity may issue digital signature certificates for statutory use without a license issued by the Central Government.

3. The Role of the Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA)

The Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA) is the apex regulatory body established under the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) by the Government of India.

🇮🇳

The Supreme National Trust Anchor: India PKI Root CA

The CCA operates the India PKI Root Certifying Authority (India PKI). The CCA's self-signed root certificate sits at the top of the entire national trust hierarchy. The CCA digitally signs the certificates of licensed intermediate CAs, legally binding all certified signatures to the Government of India.

4. Major Licensed Certifying Authorities in India

The CCA licenses a selective group of government and commercial organizations to issue digital certificates to citizens, businesses, and government departments:

  • e-Mudhra: One of the largest commercial providers of Class 3 DSCs and eSign services across India.
  • NSDL (Protean eGov Technologies): Powers tax returns, PAN services, and government administrative e-filings.
  • C-DAC (Centre for Development of Advanced Computing): The premier R&D organization under MeitY providing eSign services.
  • IDRBT (Institute for Development and Research in Banking Technology): Established by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) to issue certificates for national inter-bank payment systems like NEFT and RTGS.
  • NIC (National Informatics Centre): Issues digital certificates exclusively to government officials and ministries.

5. How the Certificate Hierarchy Works

When an e-Aadhaar is downloaded, its cryptographic validation follows a strict three-tier chain: 

[Root Anchor]      India PKI Root CA (Managed by CCA India)
                          │  (Digitally signs Intermediate CA)
[Intermediate CA]  e-Mudhra Sub-CA / NIC Sub-CA
                          │  (Digitally signs UIDAI certificate)
[End-Entity]       Unique Identification Authority of India (UIDAI)
                          │  (Digitally signs your e-Aadhaar PDF)
[Your Document]    e-Aadhaar PDF ByteRange Hash

If any link in this chain is expired, revoked, or untrusted, the verification fails.
